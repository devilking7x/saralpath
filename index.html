SaralPath — सरल पाठ

Hard textbook English, in simple words — with the Hindi meaning of every hard word, right beside it.

Built for AccessED Hacks — the “Understand” door: translation and plain-language explanations. For Hindi-medium learners who can follow a lesson but get stuck on the words.

3. Word lookup — the 93-word glossary

Every hard word in the glossary has a simple English word and a Roman Hindi meaning. Type a word to look it up.

Glossary size: … words. The glossary covers common academic words from school science, civics and maths textbooks — it is not a dictionary.

Before / after — one learner, one paragraph

Before: A Class 9 student opens her science chapter and reads: “Photosynthesis is the fundamental process by which green plants transform sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide into glucose and oxygen.” She knows “plants” and “sunlight” — but “fundamental”, “transform” and “carbon dioxide” stop her. She re-reads the sentence three times and still cannot say what it means.

After: She pastes the paragraph into SaralPath and reads: “Photosynthesis is the basic way by which green plants change sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide into glucose and oxygen.” The hard words are listed beside it with their Hindi meanings — fundamental = basic (buniyadi), transform = change (badalna). She can now explain the sentence in her own words. What still needs work: she still has to learn the science terms themselves (photosynthesis, glucose, carbon dioxide) — SaralPath simplifies the English around them, it does not teach the concepts.

Honest limits

  • This is a rule-based simplifier, not AI: a 93-word hand-built glossary plus a sentence splitter. It does not understand the text — it swaps known hard words and splits long sentences. Grammar is sometimes imperfect (the simple words are the point).
  • It simplifies the English around science terms; it does not explain the science itself. Terms like photosynthesis, glucose and carbon dioxide stay as they are — a learner still needs to learn them.
  • Reading scores (Flesch Reading Ease, Flesch-Kincaid Grade) are standard arithmetic on word and syllable counts — a useful before/after measure, not a guarantee that a particular learner will understand a particular paragraph.
  • Nothing leaves your browser. No account, no server, no tracking. Works offline once loaded.
